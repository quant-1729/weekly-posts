# Mascot inspiration: how mascot-led brands use their character, and what it means for our posts

Research note, 2026-10-15. Input for rewriting the design rules (`src/lib/design/*`), the role/action mapping (`src/lib/characters/types.ts`) and the AI prompts. Patterns are described in our own words; brand claims carry a source number from the list at the end. Numbers from vendor blogs are marked "vendor"; treat them as directional. No Instagram, X or TikTok pages were opened (logged-out access was not available), so per-brand format shares are from secondary analyses, not our own counts.

Constraints that override everything here (from `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/mascot.md`): text, logo and QR are drawn by code; posts state only facts from the brief or brand profile; regulated categories (health, finance, education) get no mascot; formal brands get none by default; sensitive occasions get no mascot.

---

## 1. Brand notes

### Duolingo (Duo, owl)
- **Formats.** Video-first: Instagram is mostly Reels, posted a few times a week, with comment replies as a standing habit [2, 3]. TikTok ran 3-5 posts a week, "only when it has original ideas" (2021) [1]. Recurring devices: Duo nagging about missed lessons, mock feuds, celebrity crushes, stunts (5-second Super Bowl spot, "Duo is dead" in Feb 2025) [1, 4, 5].
- **Mascot's job.** Duo is the content, not a logo in the corner. Design rules allow many poses and emotions; do not centre the pupils (looks unsettling); simple shapes and big eyes keep expressions readable [6]. The home-screen widget shows Duo's mood getting more frantic as midnight nears if the lesson is not done, and relaxed once it is: one character, escalating expressions, minimal data (streak + at-risk) [7].
- **Tone.** "Pushy friend" who motivates [1]; written voice is helpful, persistent, emotive, slightly awkward, never threatening or mean [6]. Ideas come from the comment section ("the comment section is your social brief") and every unhinged post still ties back to a brand truth: language learning is hard, so we make it fun [3].
- **What performed.** "Duo is dead": about 169,000 mentions in two weeks, #ripduo used 45,000+ times (Meltwater) [5]. Duolingo links viral TikToks to more sign-ups citing TikTok in its "how did you hear" survey [3]. Instagram average engagement 4.01% (vendor) [2].
- **Missteps.** In May 2025, after the "AI-first" memo backlash, Duolingo wiped/hid its TikTok and Instagram posts; reports say the TikTok account lost about 400K followers, and later jokey posts were read as dodging the issue [8]. Its own team says the unhinged style does not reach older learners and that they have commented on things they shouldn't have [3]. Lesson: humour cannot cover a real customer grievance.

### Airlearn (Air, blue cat)
- Language app spun off from Unacademy; the in-app guide is "Air" [9]. Its 2026 virality came from Air being a blunt cat that roasts learners' mistakes during AI video calls; users screen-record the calls and post them, helped by creator partnerships on TikTok [10]. Format = user-generated reaction clips, not brand-made posts.
- An Instagram account, @airlearnlanguageshow, shares language facts (about 153K followers per a search snippet, date unknown) [11].
- Nov 2025 NYC billboards used foreign words that look like English rude words, made by a creator studio to "stop people, make them laugh, teach them something" [12]. **Do not copy**: shock wordplay and roasting the user are fine for an app with a young audience; for a shop they read as insulting customers.

### Amul (Amul girl, topicals) - the closest model for us
- **Formats.** One illustrated panel per topic; began 1966 as hoardings, now "almost every day", released on digital by evening and billboards by night [13, 14]. Team of three meets Mondays, sketches 3-4 options, hand-paints [14]. A 2025 explainer puts turnaround at 24-72 hours [15].
- **Mascot's job.** The girl "takes on the subject" (dressed as the newsmaker or placed in the scene); she is in most but not all executions [14]. The headline is a pun, in English and Hindi, often bending a phrase toward butter; product presence comes through the pun and the brand line, not product claims [14, 15]. Same girl, same polka-dot frock, same hand-drawn style for decades [13, 15]: the series is recognisable before you read it.
- **Tone.** "Social observer" with gentle wit [16]. The agency balances political and religious sensitivities [14]; a 2025 analysis lists guardrails: tease all sides equally, stay away from religion, caste and sexual violence, switch to empathy in disasters [15].
- **Missteps.** Ads drew threats or removal: Indian Airlines strike, a Ganesh Chaturthi ad (Shiv Sena), Suresh Kalmadi (hoarding pulled in Pune, 2011), Mamata Banerjee (ad skipped in Kolkata), British Airways complaint (2015) [17]. Lesson for us: a small business cannot absorb that risk, so our "topical" recipe must avoid politics, religion and named people.

### Wysa (penguin)
- Mental-health chatbot whose penguin is "cute, tiny", friendly and playful and replies with GIFs and emojis [18]. Founders wanted "something empathetic, something that felt like a friend"; anonymity and "without judgement" are core [19].
- Social messages were refined with therapists and careful wording [20]. Instagram about 59.6K followers (Aug 2025) [20].
- Lesson: the **calm** dial: soft expressions, slow motion, no jokes at anyone's expense. (Health is regulated in our product, so this informs tone only; such brands get no mascot.)

### Headspace (orange dot / characters)
- No official mascot; the orange dot becomes a face, plus a cast of simple round characters [21]. Illustration and animation are "the foundation of our brand"; the team simplified characters after feedback that the brand felt "childish and cartoony" [22]. A 2024 refresh widened faces to show stress, sadness, contentment and in-between moods, with contrast and accessibility in mind [23].
- Nexus Studios has animated 150+ characters for app and social since 2016 [24]. Headspace told Instagram that illustration and animation explain how the app works and credited Instagram with a 30% lift in app conversion (self-reported) [25].
- Lesson: characters can carry abstract feelings (stress to calm) with slow, looping motion; avoid "babyish" proportions for adult audiences.

### Mailchimp (Freddie)
- Freddie "smiles, winks, and sometimes high-fives, but he does not talk"; writers must not write in his voice [26]. Humour is subtle, never condescending, and "brings our customers in on the joke"; when unsure, keep a straight face [26].
- 2018 rebrand (Collins) simplified Freddie to a single-colour mark; Cavendish Yellow leads, illustrations mostly black and white with yellow accents [27]. Brand-asset rules: generous clear space, never cluttered, always winking [28].
- Lesson: the **silent mascot** mode. The character reacts; the brand caption speaks. Useful for "Simple and respectful" tone brands.

### Brainly (Ginny) - thin evidence
- Brainly introduced Ginny in 2023 as a guide to its AI help features [29]. No public analysis of Ginny on social was found; Brainly's social is creator/UGC-led. Treat as "guide character" precedent only.

### Sleepy Owl (Indian D2C coffee)
- Blue owl holding a cup "spreading the word" across four films set in everyday coffee moments; playful, irreverent, no marketing jargon; hashtag #YourLoss ("Fear of Missing Owl") [30]. Commentators cite it as a startup reviving mascots [31]. Lesson: the mascot holds the product moment; the joke is about the customer's day, not the customer.

### Dunzo (Dunya, Harri and the "Duniverse", Indian quick commerce)
- Six mascots representing customers, partners and merchants; Reels in 2D motion, 3D and live action; humour in every Reel; consistent colour palette and shared traits tie it together [32]. Of 10-15 daily national topics, only a few are used; the marketing head says Reels beat static on reach [32].
- Harri dancing alongside a trending celebrity clip drew 2.84 lakh likes (June 2023) [33]; a 2020 Friendship Day post invited other brands' mascots to a "party" and asked followers to spot them [34]. Note: both rely on other people's audio/IP; we will not copy that.

### Aldi UK (Kevin the Carrot) - seasonal lore
- System1 reports Kevin's Christmas ads have grown more effective year after year as the character gained a family storyline; recurring characters ("fluent devices") help recognition and long-term growth [35]. Lesson: a festival "episode" each year with the same character builds memory.

## 2. Cross-brand patterns

1. **Consistency beats novelty.** Same character, style, colour and placement across years (Amul, Freddie, Kevin, Dunzo's palette) [14, 27, 32, 35].
2. **The character reacts; the text informs.** Amul puns and Duo's moods are reactions to a fact. The fact itself is short and clear.
3. **Expression carries the message at thumbnail size.** Big eyes, simple shapes, one strong emotion [6, 7].
4. **Escalation as a series.** Duo's widget moods escalate toward a deadline [7]; a countdown series can do the same, gently.
5. **Comments are the brief.** Recurring questions become posts [3].
6. **Carousels are for saving; Reels for discovery.** Metricool's 2026 sample (24M posts): carousels got ~9x the saves and over twice the reach of single images; Reels led discovery (vendor) [36].
7. **Original beats reposted.** Instagram favours original content over reposts and aggregators [37].
8. **Humour has a ceiling.** Every brand that pushed it (Duo, Amul, Airlearn) has a list of incidents [3, 8, 17].

## B. Composition rules for the engine

1. **Scale bands** (character height / canvas height): Lead (celebrator, sign_holder, presenter in greetings) 40-55%. Support (presenter, pointer, narrator) 25-40%. Accent (peeker, percher) 12-25%. Story 9:16 uses the same bands of the safe area, not the full canvas.
2. **Safe areas.** Keep the character and all text out of the top ~14% and bottom ~20% of stories/Reels covers (platform UI). On squares keep a 6% margin except for deliberate frame-breaks.
3. **One focal point.** Rank: headline/sign > character face > supporting text > logo. If the owner's photo is present, it becomes #1 and the character drops to the accent or support band.
4. **Eye-line and gesture toward the message.** Character faces the headline (or the QR/fact it points at). Never face out of the canvas, except the peeker looking in from the edge.
5. **Text and character never collide.** No overlap between character and text glyphs; minimum gap = 3% of canvas width. Bubble tail points to the mouth, bubble sits on the side of the face away from the edge.
6. **Frame-breaking is intentional only.** Peeker: 30-50% of the body cropped by one edge. Percher: sits on a text card edge, overlapping the card by 10-15% of its own height. Nobody else is cropped.
7. **Negative space.** At least 30% of the canvas free of text, character and logo. If text overflows, shrink character to the next band before shrinking type.
8. **Brand colour.** Brand primary appears in the character's accent (scarf etc.) and one text element; background is a brand tint or the abstract AI background; accent colour for confetti/props only. Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1; existing halo rule on dark backgrounds.
9. **Series consistency.** A series (recipes 6, 14, carousels) locks character side, scale band, role, background family and type style. Only the action/expression changes, as in Duo's mood set [7].
10. **Expression readable at thumbnail.** Choose actions whose silhouette reads at 200 px (arms away from body). Avoid *idle* and *loading* in posts; avoid *sleep* except for "closed today".
11. **One character per post** unless a carousel needs two moods (same character twice in before/after only).

## C. Copy and voice rules for the AI

1. **Two voices.** The bubble is the character reacting (first person, feeling, present tense). The caption is the business speaking (facts, offer, CTA). Facts (price, date, offer, contact) go on the sign/headline/caption, never only in the bubble.
2. **Bubble length.** ≤ 8 words, max 2 lines, no hashtags, no emojis inside bubbles; one bubble per post. Allow **silent mode** (Freddie): no bubble, reaction only. Default silent for "Simple and respectful" tone and for festival greetings.
3. **Personality dials** (default by character, owner can change):
   - *Playful*: Parchi, sticky note, myna bird, baby elephant, Tween. Puns allowed, light exaggeration, exclamation ≤ 1.
   - *Helpful*: compass, letter, relay baton, tiffin box, capsule. Plain, warm, "here's how". No jokes about the product's quality.
   - *Calm*: ribbon bookmark, cloth bundle, thread spool. Short, gentle, no exclamations (Wysa/Headspace register).
4. **Humour guardrails.** Never mock or roast customers, competitors or staff (Airlearn/Duo-style roasting is out). No guilt, threats or fake urgency. No politics, religion, caste, tragedies, named public figures, other brands or their mascots. No sexual innuendo or lookalike rude words. When unsure, no joke [26].
5. **No invented facts.** The bubble and puns must not imply new claims ("best in town", "fastest", ratings). Any adjective of superiority not in the brief → "please check" flag.
6. **No borrowed culture.** No meme templates, film dialogues, song lines or trend audio; topical posts reference the moment in our own words.
7. **Language.** Bubble follows the post language; Hinglish/code-mix only if the brand already writes that way.

## D. Carousel blueprint (6 slides; 5 or 7 by dropping/adding a body slide)

| Slide | Purpose | Mascot role / action | Text |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Cover hook | Stop the scroll, promise the payoff | presenter or pointer at title; *surprised* (+) or *point-right* (toward swipe) | Title ≤ 7 words + "Swipe" cue |
| 2 Problem / question | Name the customer's situation | narrator; *think* (+) | ≤ 15 words |
| 3 Point 1 | First fact/tip | pointer at text; *point-left/right* | Heading + ≤ 20 words |
| 4 Point 2 | Second fact/tip | percher on text card; *perch* | Heading + ≤ 20 words |
| 5 Proof from the brief | Offer, USP, rating, timing (only from brief) | sign_holder; *hold-sign* | Sign = the fact verbatim |
| 6 CTA | What to do now | presenter beside contact/QR; *point-down* or *wave* | CTA ≤ 5 words + contact block |

Rules: same side and scale band on slides 2-5; the character moves across the series (left on 2, right on 3) only if the engine keeps the text column fixed; slide 1 and 6 may use the Lead band. Caption repeats the key points for people who don't swipe.

## E. What NOT to copy

- **Brand IP.** No owls, blue cats, polka-dot girls, winking chimps, orange dot faces, or look-alikes; no "Amul-style" lettering or layout clones; no tagline puns on someone else's brand name.
- **Unhinged tone.** Menace, roasting and stunts (fake deaths, wiped feeds) work for a funded app with a huge young audience and a social team on watch; for a shop they cost trust and cannot be moderated weekly [3, 8].
- **Politics and news satire.** Amul has decades of goodwill and still gets threats [17].
- **Platform risk.** Business accounts are limited to Meta's royalty-free library; trending songs are not cleared for commercial use and short clips are not safe [38, 39]. Instagram reduces reach for reposted/aggregated content [37]. So our future animated loops use our own or library-cleared audio only, and we never remix others' clips (Dunzo/Harri-style).
- **Other brands' mascots.** No "mascot party" tagging other brands without permission.
- **AI realism.** Our characters stay code-drawn and flat; no AI-rendered mascot likenesses or realistic people.

### Note for later animated loops (from Duo, Headspace, Dunzo)
2-6 s seamless loops: one action, start and end on the same pose; first frame already shows the character mid-gesture with the headline visible (no blank intro); *wave*, *celebrate*, *point*, *remind*, *jump* loop well; calm brands use slow *nod*/*idle* breathing [6, 7, 22, 32].
